Talent doesn't have a zip code. Access does.

HBCUs produce extraordinary athletes, but the path from campus to a front-office, agency, marketing, or league job is far narrower than the path onto the field. Many students never meet a single person working in sports business, so they don't know the roles exist, let alone how to get them.

## Where the pipeline narrows

- **Exposure.** Students can't aim for jobs they've never seen.
- **Networks.** Hiring in sports still runs on relationships, and those start with access to the room.
- **Preparation.** Knowing what to study, which experience counts, and how to ask matters as much as talent.

## Why it matters to the industry

Sports audiences are diverse. The people building the products, partnerships, and policies should be too. More HBCU voices means better decisions, stronger cultural fluency, and a wider talent pool.

## What widens the pipeline

Events that put students beside professionals, mentorship that continues after the panel ends, and employers who show up on campus. That is the work Behind the Game exists to do.
